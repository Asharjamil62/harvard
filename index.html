Neurobiology

Harvard researchers are exploring the complexities of the brain and the
nervous system, and uncovering ways to treat and prevent diseases like
dementia, multiple sclerosis, and ALS.

Supporting research

Harvard recently announced an investment of $150 million—one of
the largest direct investments in faculty research in Harvard’s
history—to support the advancement of promising areas of
scientific research.

Demystifying the migraine

Michael Moskowitz, Harvard Medical School professor of neurology,
has made multiple discoveries that have revolutionized thinking about
migraines, deepened knowledge about why some treatments work, and
led to new treatments that are available and prescribed now.

Uncovering Epstein-Barr’s connection to multiple sclerosis

Harvard researchers published a landmark study confirming the Epstein-Barr virus as the leading cause of multiple sclerosis, and found that healthy diets in midlife may benefit long-term cognitive health.

Reversing the course of ALS

Merit Cudkowicz, a Harvard Medical School professor of neurology, co-authored a paper on a new drug that can radically slow and even
reverse the course of the disease in a small subset of patients with a
rare genetic variant.
